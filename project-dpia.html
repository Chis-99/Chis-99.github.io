The Challenge

A fictional Jamaican educational institution planned to replace fragmented paper and digital processes with a centralized, cloud-based Student Information System.

The proposed platform would process substantial amounts of personal information belonging to students, parents, guardians and staff, including academic records, attendance data, disciplinary records, financial information and health-related information.

Because the system processes higher-risk information involving children and relies on external cloud service providers, a DPIA was conducted before production deployment.

My Role

For this simulated engagement, I performed the role of a privacy and cybersecurity GRC analyst.

  • Identified personal-data processing activities
  • Created a personal-data inventory
  • Mapped internal and third-party data flows
  • Assessed privacy and security risks
  • Evaluated technical and administrative controls
  • Performed third-party vendor due diligence
  • Developed a retention and disposal schedule
  • Mapped compliance requirements
  • Assessed inherent and residual risk
  • Developed a remediation roadmap
  • Prepared the final DPIA report

Assessment Methodology

  1. Defined the system and processing scope
  2. Identified data subjects and personal-data categories
  3. Documented processing purposes
  4. Mapped internal and external data flows
  5. Assessed necessity and data minimization
  6. Identified privacy and security risks
  7. Scored risk using a 5 × 5 likelihood × impact methodology
  8. Identified existing and recommended controls
  9. Calculated residual risk
  10. Evaluated third-party/cloud-provider risk
  11. Mapped relevant compliance requirements
  12. Developed remediation recommendations
  13. Issued an assessment decision

Privacy Risk Assessment

Fifteen privacy and security risks were evaluated using a 5 × 5 likelihood × impact methodology.

  • Unauthorized access to student records
  • Excessive staff privileges
  • Weak authentication and account compromise
  • Former employees retaining system access
  • Excessive retention of personal data
  • Accidental disclosure through email
  • Cloud-provider security incidents
  • Inadequate breach-response processes
  • Cross-border data processing risks

Third-Party Vendor Assessment

A 42-question privacy and security due-diligence assessment was completed against a fictional cloud provider.

Weighted Readiness Score: 70%

Vendor Decision: Conditional Approval

Key concerns included incomplete hosting and support-location information, cross-border transfer safeguards, subprocessor transparency, privileged-access evidence, audit logging, breach-notification terms and vendor-exit planning.

Compliance & Framework Mapping

Eighteen privacy and security requirements were mapped against current controls, evidence, identified gaps and remediation actions.

Jamaica Data Protection Act NIST CSF 2.0 NIST Privacy Framework ISO/IEC 27001

Framework mappings demonstrate control alignment and do not represent certification or a legal compliance determination.

Key Findings

  • Cross-border hosting and support locations require further validation.
  • Vendor subprocessor transparency requires improvement.
  • Retention periods require legal and policy validation.
  • Privacy-breach response processes require formalization.
  • Role-based access controls require implementation and testing.
  • Vendor exit and deletion obligations require stronger controls.
  • Security-control implementation requires operating evidence.

Assessment Outcome

Conditional Approval for Controlled Implementation

The proposed Student Information System demonstrates a viable privacy and security design; however, material governance, vendor, transfer, retention and control-evidence gaps remain.

The system may proceed with configuration, control implementation and testing using synthetic or non-production information.

Live student personal data should not be processed until the critical remediation items identified in the DPIA have been addressed and formally reviewed.
