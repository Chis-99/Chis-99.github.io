How I Approach the Work

I try to approach cybersecurity from both the technical and organizational sides. A vulnerability or control gap matters because of what it could mean for the systems, information, people and business processes that depend on it.

That means my work usually begins with understanding the environment before jumping directly to a framework or checklist. I want to understand what the organization is trying to protect, what could realistically go wrong and what evidence supports the assessment.

From there, I focus on producing deliverables that make the next decision easier — whether that is a risk register, control assessment, treatment plan, privacy assessment, incident-response document or executive summary.

Principles That Guide My Work

01

Understand Before Assessing

Security decisions should begin with the business, information, systems and dependencies involved.

02

Make Risk Traceable

Findings should connect clearly to assets, threats, controls, owners and treatment decisions.

03

Use Evidence

A control should not be considered effective simply because documentation says that it exists.

04

Communicate Clearly

Cybersecurity analysis is most useful when technical findings can be understood by the people responsible for making decisions.
